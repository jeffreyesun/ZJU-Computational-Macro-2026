\section*{Course Overview}

These notes are a self-study introduction to computational methods for
solving macroeconomic models with dynamics, heterogeneous agents, and
varying assumptions about expectations. The vehicle is Julia: every
lecture builds and runs the model it discusses. Starting from a
single-agent consumption--savings model, the materials build up to
representative-agent general equilibrium, then heterogeneous-agent
general equilibrium, and finally to the ``Continuation Value is All You
Need'' (CVIAYN) method for solving heterogeneous-agent dynamic models
with aggregate uncertainty, as in Sun (2026).

The package consists of eight paired deliverables: a slide deck and a
tutorial notebook for each lecture. The notebooks are designed to be
run cell-by-cell; the slides serve as a guided reading companion. Code
appearing in the slides is verbatim from the matching notebook.

\section*{Materials}

No textbooks are required. The slide decks and tutorial notebooks are
the full set of materials.

\textbf{L3}
& \textbf{Heterogeneity.} Reading the grid-based solution as a
  description of a population. Solving for agents heterogeneous in
  dimensions beyond wealth. \\

\textbf{L4}
& \textbf{Equilibrium.} Heterogeneous agents in equilibrium: the
  Aiyagari model. Recursive equilibrium and its computation. \\

\textbf{L5}
& \textbf{Transitions.} Perfect-foresight transition dynamics in
  Aiyagari. \\

\textbf{L6}
& \textbf{Spatial Heterogeneity and Calibration.} A spatial Aiyagari
  model with Gumbel migration; calibrating preference shifters to
  population data by indirect inference. \\

\textbf{L7}
& \textbf{Uncertainty.} The Krusell--Smith model. Aggregate shocks,
  the state-space explosion, and the simulation-and-regression
  workaround. \\

\textbf{L8}
& \textbf{Continuation Value is All You Need.} Parameterizing the
  continuation value with a neural network and training on the outer
  Bellman residual. \\
